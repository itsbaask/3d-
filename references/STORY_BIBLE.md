# DOLA Shorts — Visual & Comedy Bible

## Brand promise
A clean, instantly readable, silent 3D slapstick world: expressive characters, clever physical cause-and-effect, warm desert atmosphere, satisfying payoffs, and replayable endings. The comedy should feel designed, not random.

## Visual language
- **Shape readability:** each character silhouette should remain distinct at phone size.
- **Material consistency:** maintain the reference's material language; avoid random shifts between plastic, fur, feather, clay or metal.
- **Lighting:** coherent sun direction, readable faces, grounded contact shadows and controlled highlights.
- **Palette:** derive the palette from DESERT_WORLD_MASTER.png; do not arbitrarily recolor the environment between shots.
- **Environment:** use sand, rocks, dunes and existing world cues as purposeful staging, not decorative clutter.
- **Camera:** stable and motivated; movement reveals information or heightens a gag, never distracts from it.

## Character protection
Use each master image as the authority for identity. Preserve face, eyes, body/head proportions, colors, silhouette, materials and distinguishing features. Do not invent personality details that contradict visible design. Never replace a character with a generic lookalike. Use only the characters required for the gag.

## Silent comedy principles
1. Show a want before explaining an obstacle.
2. Let the audience see the cause before the consequence.
3. Make the character's intention readable through gaze, posture and hand movement.
4. Escalate by changing one variable, not by adding unrelated chaos.
5. Make the reversal surprising but fair in hindsight.
6. Let the payoff breathe long enough for the audience to register it.
7. End on a visual rhyme with the opening, ideally with a changed meaning.

## Continuity rules
- Track which hand holds the prop and where it is after each beat.
- Preserve screen direction, foot placement, shadows and relative character scale.
- Do not teleport props or reset damage/positions between cuts.
- Use consistent time of day and light direction unless a visible story event motivates a change.
- Avoid crowding the vertical frame with all characters at once.

## Shot-design checklist
- Is the goal visible in the first 7 seconds?
- Can the audience tell who acts, what moves, and why?
- Does the camera show the cause and effect in the same readable view when possible?
- Is the lead's face visible at the emotional reaction?
- Does each cut add information?
- Is the payoff legible without sound?
- Does the ending echo the opening?

## Hard prohibitions
No dialogue or voice-over, no visible text/subtitles/logos/watermarks, no identity drift, no unsafe imitation, no cruel or graphic harm, no random character duplication, and no claim that a prompt or ZIP is a rendered video.
